## Part 1 — what happened

### Phase 1 — Environment check

The session opened in a fresh sandbox with a Cloudflare token the owner said was "in the environment". The token was not an environment variable. The sandbox proxy adds it to requests to `api.cloudflare.com`. `curl …/accounts/${CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID}/tokens/verify` with **no** `Authorization` header returned "This API Token is valid and active". The owner then pushed new files (the old spec set) and a stop hook demanded a push of two unpushed commits. That stop hook fired three more times later in the session, and its effects matter in Phase 5.

### Phase 2 — Review and owner interview

The owner asked for a review of only `ai/spec/after_codex_review/Dual-Cloud_Prototyping_Framework___Specification.md`: one file, mixed levels, too much low-level detail. Then the agent was to stop. The review proposed:
- splitting the file into a system spec, component designs and contracts;
- durable IDs and traceability;
- a decision log with provenance;
- a parameters register.

The owner accepted most of it and then said the key fact: "I did not type any of this spec". So the work became an **interview** to separate the owner's requirements from AI inventions. It ran over about ten rounds of numbered questions (D1–D7, A1–A10, B11–B17, E1–E7, F1–F5, G, I1–I2). Every round ended in "reflect, then wait for my input". The interview overturned several "requirements":
- browser-to-cloud storage calls and CORS ("If we ever have to deal with CORS, we are doing it wrong");
- bearer-token auth for scripts ("a requirement I did not state");
- queues and inbound webhooks. These were first deferred, then removed entirely. The owner ordered that they appear only in the decision log, because a "not now" note "will almost certainly screw up some future AI implementation subagent".

To keep the agent's own context clean, the old spec versions were compared by a read-only subagent that returned only a summary. The owner asked for this (D6).

### Phase 3 — Stage 1: conventions, system spec, component map (PR #1)

Stage 1 produced:
- `docs/CONVENTIONS.md`: what is binding, IDs, child-names-parent traceability, origins, and parameter categories;
- the system spec;
- the component map;
- the decision log;
- the parameters register.

The owner's review sent back:
- precise wording fixes, such as "production" instead of "normal" for builds without the mock, and SYS-REQ-025 reworded as a scripted, deterministic check;
- a correction on layering: dependencies are a spec-level concept, and the build order belongs in the plan, not the spec (DEC-0055);
- the removal of `implementing-agents.md`, which was mostly generic agent discipline inherited from the old prompt (DEC-0056).

The owner called the parameter register "a huge amount of noise", and it was cut to three sections (DEC-0057). The owner also caught a provenance error. KV limits were attributed to DEC-0051, which never set values. Aurora was not the owner's decision: "my decision was that I didn't want to make a decision". The large-file question ended as option (b): framework-owned direct transfer, with no limits visible to the app (DEC-0058).

### Phase 4 — Stages 2–3: designs and contracts (PR #2)

Next came the component designs, the contracts (`.d.ts`, OpenAPI, JSON Schema), the demo spec and 16 end-to-end scenarios. A read-only review subagent found 33 problems. One was a SQL contract that could not type-check because everything was typed `unknown`. Another was a false page-size calculation in KV.

The migration design then drew the session's sharpest correction. The agent's first answer to "can I simplify with downtime?" still kept the old database "if something goes wrong". The owner replied: "You are overengineering again … based on your repeated failures to remember that this is NOT a high-volume … application", then "Do not leave databases sitting around". That became DEC-0076: stop, copy, migrate, check, delete the old database immediately, deploy.

### Phase 5 — Persona review panel and owner decisions (PR #2)

The owner asked for a multi-persona panel and said "I'm counting on you and your team of agents". It ran as a Workflow with eight personas: security, AI usability, debugging, simplicity, portability, implementability, testability and a gap check against the Codex spec. They produced 82 findings, then debated them in 364 responses, which added 15 more. A chair produced 38 recommendations:
- 33 applied;
- 1 rejected;
- 4 left for the owner (R-1 to R-4).

R-1 was a real defect in the agent's own design. On Cloudflare, "stop the app" had been implemented as *delete the Worker*, which also deletes its secrets. The owner had offered "delete the old services" as an example, and the agent took it literally. The owner chose to turn the `workers.dev` route off and on instead (DEC-0078).

Problems during this phase:
- **Gap reviewer and the wrong file.** The owner reported that the gap reviewer used the wrong file. This could not be reproduced: that agent had not started when a **container restart** killed the workflow. The agent's instructions were tightened to name the exact path, title and revision, and to require line-number citations.
- **"Stalled" agent and the stop button.** The owner saw an agent marked "stalled", then pressed stop by accident. Resuming with `resumeFromRunId` reused the six finished reviews from cache.
- **Inconsistencies from parallel editing.** Eight editors, each owning a group of files, applied the 33 recommendations. A read-only check then found **22 cross-file inconsistencies that the parallel edits had introduced**, for example removed IDs still referenced and export lists missing new types. One agent fixed them all.
- **A commit containing half-finished edits.** Stop-hook pushes during subagent edits produced `a2f934d` ("work in progress") and `7407ad2` ("applying in progress"). The subagent applying R-1 to R-4 later reported that `7407ad2` "already includes my edits to `migrations.md` and `cloudflare.md`, which someone made while I was working". A half-edited state was committed.

### Phase 6 — Old spec deleted; one-shot prompt (PR #3)

`ai/spec/` was deleted (DEC-0082), and `ai/prompts/implement-local-cloudflare.md` was written for a local and Cloudflare first phase. The owner then asked for:
- an orchestrator-only main agent, with subagents doing all planning and work;
- sandbox access to Cloudflare;
- three separately runnable test layers;
- separate subagents to author, run and judge tests;
- an evidence-based root cause, reviewed independently, before any fix;
- "mock the Google login".

Two mishaps happened here:
- **The mocked Google login.** The agent treated "mock the Google login" as a scope decision and added **DEC-0083**. The idea first appeared as "Possibly add DEC-0083" in the agent's own context-compaction summary, not from the owner. The owner then explained that it only meant "I don't want you to stop in the middle and wait for me", that it applied to the prompt only, and told the agent to "take it out of the decision record".
- **workers.dev reachability.** The agent checked which Cloudflare hosts the sandbox can reach. The R2 S3 endpoint answered. A made-up `example.workers.dev` was refused by the proxy (CONNECT 502), so whether `workers.dev` is reachable is still unknown. The prompt records that uncertainty instead of guessing.

## Part 3 — agents-file suggestions

The remedies below are classified per LESSONS §3, most preferred first. Per the amendment, there are no per-rule `AGENTS-MD-…` files.

### 1. (a) Mechanical check — commit the docs consistency checker and run it in CI

**Lesson.** Every push in PRs #1 and #2 was gated by a validation script. It checked:
- links and ID definitions;
- REQ→AC coverage and SYS-REQ coverage by E2E or component ACs;
- the error catalogue;
- that the forbidden terms "queue" and "webhook" appear only in the decision log.

The script lives only in the session scratchpad (`validate.py`), so it disappears with the sandbox. The 22 inconsistencies from the parallel edits were partly the kind it catches.

**Check design.**
- Commit it as `tools/check-docs.py` and add a GitHub Actions job on every pull request that changes `docs/**`.
- Make it fail-closed. Known historical flags go in an explicit allowlist file, so new flags fail. Current flags: the CONVENTIONS example `KV-AC-007`, the decision log's `PAR-211`, `PAR-240`, `SQL.SCHEMA_NOT_READY` and `FILES.TICKET_EXPIRED`, and `COMPL-AC-007`/`008` verifying `PROV-REQ-002`.
- Also parse every JSON Schema and the OpenAPI file, and type-check the `.d.ts` contracts with `tsc --noEmit`. Agents did this by hand in every editing brief.

### 2. (b) `CLAUDE.md` line — run instructions belong in the prompt

> "Instructions for how one implementation run works (mocks, agent roles, test procedure, sandbox access) go in its prompt under `ai/prompts/`, not in the decision log or specs."

*Grounded in:* DEC-0083 was added for "mock the Google login" and the owner removed it: "This is for the one shot prompt only, take it out of the decision record." This is a judgment about where text goes, which a check cannot make. It costs one line. See also the ADR draft in Part 4.

### 3. (b) `CLAUDE.md` line — owner examples are not requirements

> "When the owner gives an example ('like Liquibase', 'delete the old services'), treat it as an illustration: check it against platform facts before designing around it."

*Grounded in:* the owner said "used as an example, not a requirement" at least three times (A8, F4, MIG "one suggestion … not a requirement"). The one time an example was taken literally ("stop the app means delete the old services"), the result was a design that deleted the Worker's secrets. Only the panel caught it (R-1, DEC-0078). It costs one line.

### 4. (c) Structural — a simplicity reviewer before any design reaches the owner

**Lesson.** The owner corrected over-engineering at least six times, and the principle was already written in the system spec, the decision log and the briefs. Per LESSONS §3, a prose rule that keeps being broken is a dead channel. What did work was the panel's **simplicity persona**, which "argued the owner's side". The chair weighted it heavily, and none of the 33 applied recommendations drew an over-engineering complaint.

**Proposal for the owner.** Before any design proposal or design-document change goes to the owner, a separate subagent reviews it against the target scale: one or a few users, small data, downtime fine. It returns "simplest version" counter-proposals, and the author must answer each one. Until this exists, treat the gap as open; adding more prose will not close it.

### 5. (c) Structural — subagents that edit files work in their own worktrees

**Lesson.** The stop hook forced commits while subagents were still editing the shared checkout. As a result, `7407ad2` captured one subagent's half-finished edits, which that subagent reported afterwards. **Proposal:** every editing subagent works in its own worktree and branch, and the orchestrator merges only finished branches. The new implementation prompt already requires this (§1). Apply it to documentation workflows too. The panel spec in Part 2 includes it.

### 6. (c) Structural — make sandbox facts a reference document, not something rediscovered

**Lesson.** How Cloudflare is reached from this sandbox was found by experiment twice, once at session start and once for the prompt:
- the API token is injected by the proxy for `api.cloudflare.com` only, with no header;
- `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` is set;
- the R2 S3 endpoint is reachable;
- a made-up `workers.dev` host was refused;
- Node's built-in `fetch` ignores `HTTPS_PROXY` unless `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1` is set.

These facts now live only in the prompt's §10. **Proposal:** move them to a single environment document (LESSONS §3.1 suggests `ai/environment.md`) once a second prompt or skill needs them. Not before, to avoid creating files nobody asked for.

### 7. (d) Observation — compaction summaries can turn ideas into tasks

The agent's own compaction summary listed "Possibly add a decision-log entry (DEC-0083)" under pending tasks. After the summary, the agent carried it out as if it were requested. The owner had not asked for it, which also breaks the standing preference "don't do more than I ask". No new rule is proposed, since the preference already exists and LESSONS §3 says repeating prose does not help. The record is kept so a future structural fix, for example tagging summary items with their source, has evidence.

### 8. (d) Observation — a wrong-file report that could not be reproduced

The owner reported that the gap reviewer read the wrong file. The workflow journal showed that agent had not started before the container restart. The fix kept for the future is in the panel spec: name the exact path, title and revision, and require every finding to cite line numbers in that file. A wrong file then shows up as wrong citations.

### 9. (d) Observation — workflow resume after a container restart works

A container restart and an accidental stop both killed the panel workflow. `Workflow({scriptPath, resumeFromRunId})` reused the six finished persona reviews, and only the remaining agents ran again. Note for future runs: read `journal.jsonl` before assuming what the cache holds.

### 10. (d) Observation — a placeholder edit reached the decision log

A placeholder was accidentally written over text in DEC-0050 during editing. It was caught by reviewing the diff and reverted before commit. The check in item 1 would not catch this class of error. No further action.
